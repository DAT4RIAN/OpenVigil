# Examples — Positive Reference Corpus

Twenty-two real pages (9 brand + 8 dashboards + 1 AI console + 4 H5), each a clean instance of one soul + one hero engine (brand register), one dashboard morphology (product register), or one phone morphology (h5 register). Read them to see the substrate, soul, and engine — or the layout shell, chart-crafting, and reduced-motion rules, or the phone frame and its native furniture — applied together in shipped code, **not as templates to copy verbatim**.

**All 22 are self-contained and runnable.** No remote assets, no image files: the dashboards inline their avatars/photos as data-URIs, the H5 pages generate their imagery from a `PH()` SVG placeholder function, and the 9 brand pages generate every visual in code (canvas, WebGL, SVG, CSS geometry). Libraries, where used at all, come from the bundled `lib/`. Four of the brand pages ship **zero JS libraries**. Opening any file in a browser renders and animates it.

> **Why no photographs anywhere in this corpus?** Because assets are the one thing that doesn't travel. A recipe travels; a 4 MB JPEG doesn't. Pages whose craft depends on real photography — a photographic hero, a mask-dissolved product shot, a lookbook — are **not** bundled here; their techniques live as paste-ready recipes in `../references/page-crafting.md` (§2 image composition, §2.B the photographic-hero scrim stack) instead. When your brief *does* imply real imagery, that's `../references/asset-sourcing.md`'s job — and shipping zero imagery on an image-implied brief is a bug, not minimalism (`anti-cheap.md`).

For a wider bench of technique notes beyond these files, `../references/inspiration-catalog.md` catalogues more pages by persona.

## Brand register — read these as COORDINATES, not as templates

> **This table is the antidote to the thing examples otherwise cause.** An open example file exerts a strong pull: the model mirrors its section order, its card counts, its shell — and every page converges. So each row below is annotated with **where it sits on the five divergence axes** (`../references/divergence.md` §3). Use it in two moves:
>
> 1. **Open the closest example** and lift the *craft primitives* — the motion gate, the DPR math, the scrim stack, the coordinate normalization. Those **should** be identical on every page; they are the floor.
> 2. **Then find your brief on this table and deliberately move off it.** If your draw lands on the same palette + layout + material as a row here, you are about to rebuild it. Change an axis on purpose.
>
> **Never lift the section sequence, the card count, or the shell.** Those are the page's identity, and copying them is exactly how nine examples become one look.

## H5 register (phone morphology)

Four pages that only ever live on a phone — `../references/h5-mobile.md`. They cover the two morphologies you will be asked for most (**A · app shell**, three of them, and **D · commerce stack**) and, between them, the entire native-furniture set: fake status bar, floating pill TabBar, center FAB, bottom sheet, home indicator, sticky buy bar, scrim, and the `:active` press states that replace hover.

**Read the frame first, then the content.** The value of these four files is mostly in the first 80 lines of CSS — the viewport meta, the locked `body` + scrolling child, the `@media (min-width: 560px)` phone frame, and the `env(safe-area-inset-*)` math. That part is **near-identical across all four and should be identical in yours**; it's the floor, like the motion gate and the DPR math elsewhere in this corpus. What you diverge on is the palette, the type, the metaphor, and the chart language — never the furniture (`h5-mobile.md` §11).

**Three of the four are app shells, and that is the point.** `pawpal`, `fern` and `peach` are the same morphology (A), the same frame, the same TabBar contract — and they do not look like each other at all: true-black + coral, warm sand + ochre, pastel gradient + peach. Put them side by side before you build; they are the corpus's clearest demonstration that **rotation happens in the soul, not in the furniture**.

| File | Morphology · wraps | What to study |
|------|--------------------|----------------|
| `h5-pawpal-app-shell.html` | **A · app shell** · wraps `product` | The complete native-app skeleton: fake status bar with a *real* clock, floating pill TabBar (`blur(22px)`, 46px round buttons, **filled** active state) with a raised center FAB, white bottom sheet (`translateY(102%)`, grab handle, tappable scrim), home indicator. Note its reduced-motion block **removes** `.fly` rather than freezing it (§7) |

**The placeholder convention.** `pawpal`, `brew` and `fern` replace every photograph with `PH(hue)` — a gradient SVG data-URI built in ~10 lines at the top of the `<script>`, applied via `data-ph="25"` on the markup or `PH(78)` inside a data array. The `<img>` tags and all their `object-fit` / `filter` / `border-radius` CSS are **unchanged from the shipped originals**, so swapping `PH(n)` for a real URL is the whole migration. That is deliberate: the layout rules for a photo are the transferable part; the photo isn't. (`peach` needs none — it ships zero images.)

> **One trap inside that convention**, and it bit all three files: the SVG is passed through `encodeURIComponent`, so a gradient reference written as `fill="url(%23g)"` gets double-escaped to `%2523` and the fill silently fails — you get the decorative circles on a transparent ground and no gradient, which looks *plausible* and is why it survived. Write `url(#g)` and let `encodeURIComponent` do the escaping.

**One honest note on these.** `h5-brew` uses `#fff` as its page background, and `pawpal` / `fern` / `peach` each carry untinted neutrals — all trip the detector's P2 "pure `#fff`/`#000`" rule (`design-dna.md` §3); `fern` also names tokens `--sand` / `--card`, which trips the default-category-palette-token rule, and `peach` scatters ~40 colour literals outside `:root`. They are kept **as shipped** rather than retouched, because a light page is where those rules are most often broken in the wild and you should see the real trade-off, not a cleaned-up one. **If you're building fresh, tint the neutrals and lift the literals into tokens.**

### Not in the corpus (know this before you go looking)

Apart from `relay`, every product-register example here is a **dashboard** — a page you *read*. There is **no form-workflow page**: no publish wizard, no merchant console, no settings or config page, no review queue. If that's what you're building (`product-ui.md` §1's workflow shell), **do not force-fit a dashboard example onto it** — the shells differ, and the failure modes differ (a dashboard fails by being unreadable; a console fails by being *unfinishable*). Build from **`../references/workflow-ui.md`**, which carries the recipes in prose: the rail + capped form column + sticky aside, numbered section cards, the radio-card choice, the live-preview pane, the pre-submit check card, derived budget panels, draft/commit.

**Four of the six H5 morphologies also have no example here.** The corpus covers **A** (app shell) and **D** (commerce stack). There is no **B** paged deck (活动页), no **C** snap narrative (报告 H5), no **E** longform mobile site, and no **F** ambient screen (weather/poster). Build those from `../references/h5-mobile.md` §4, which carries each one's mechanics in prose — the three-input deck handler with its wheel cooldown, the `scroll-snap-stop: always` narrative, the sticky-header longform site, the two-layer scene crossfade with per-scene veils. **Do not force-fit the app shell onto a 活动页**: one is a container you navigate, the other is a sequence you advance through, and they fail in different ways.

**There is no phone AI console either.** `relay` is the desktop form; `ai-console.md` §9 is the phone form, and it has no example. What the three app shells here *do* give you is the half of it that is furniture — the frame, the safe areas, the TabBar, the sheet, the press states. Build the other half from §9: the pinned run strip under the status bar, the stream as the whole scroll container, evidence in a sheet rather than inline, and **停止 living in the thumb bar**. Do not reach for `relay`'s three columns inside 390px; the desktop layout is the one thing §9 explicitly replaces.

The "open the closest example first" rule in `product-ui.md` §0 means *study shipped code of the thing you're building*. When there is no close example, it doesn't fire.

**Note:** these are single-file pages. All are runnable from disk — GSAP, where used, loads from the bundled `lib/gsap.min.js` (+ `lib/ScrollTrigger.min.js`); `caliber`, `flux` and `morph` ship **zero JS libraries**; the four H5 pages and `relay` ship zero libraries and load only Google Fonts; `aether` is the one exception and still reaches for an external `three.module.js`, so treat it as read-only. In your own project, install GSAP properly rather than copying the `lib/` path (`chart-crafting.md` §6).

**Every page here passes `scripts/detect.mjs`** — no P0 findings. That is the bar for the corpus: an example that violates the rules teaches the violation, because a positive reference is copied far more literally than a prose rule is followed. If you add a page here, run the detector on it first. And a clean detector run is still only the regex layer — it cannot see whether the soul is distinct or the engine renders real pixels. Look at the page.

**Lift patterns, not whole files.** Each new brief deserves its own soul.
